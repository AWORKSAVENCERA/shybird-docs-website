Governance

Purpose

This document provides an overview of the governance, risk management, compliance and change management processes that support the delivery of our Shybird product suite.

The objective of these processes is to ensure that services are operated effectively, risks are managed appropriately, and changes are introduced in a controlled manner while supporting security, reliability, compliance and customer trust.

This document is intended for customers, prospective customers and auditors undertaking procurement, due diligence or assurance activities.

Scope

This document applies to:

  • SaaS products and services
  • Production and supporting environments
  • Service delivery activities
  • Information security controls
  • Compliance activities
  • Organisational governance processes
  • Third-party suppliers that materially support service delivery

Governance framework

We maintain a governance framework that establishes accountability for the management, operation and continual improvement of our services.

The framework is designed to:

  • Support strategic objectives
  • Manage operational and compliance risks
  • Protect customer information
  • Ensure regulatory obligations are met
  • Promote effective decision-making
  • Support continual improvement

Governance activities include regular review of:

  • Risks and mitigations
  • Security performance
  • Compliance obligations
  • Service performance
  • Major incidents
  • Significant changes
  • Customer feedback
  • Audit findings

Roles and responsibilities

Defined roles and responsibilities exist for the management of governance activities.

Typical responsibilities include:

Executive leadership

Responsible for:

  • Strategic direction
  • Risk oversight
  • Regulatory accountability
  • Resource allocation
  • Approval of major initiatives

Product management

Responsible for:

  • Product strategy and roadmap
  • Prioritisation of enhancements
  • Customer needs and feedback
  • Product lifecycle management

Engineering and operations

Responsible for:

  • Service reliability
  • Security implementation
  • Change delivery
  • Platform performance
  • Operational resilience

Information security and compliance

Responsible for:

  • Security governance
  • Compliance monitoring
  • Risk assessments
  • Policy management
  • Audit coordination

Risk management

Risk management approach

We maintain a risk management process designed to identify, assess, manage and monitor risks that could affect service delivery, customer data, regulatory compliance or business operations.

Risk management activities include:

  • Risk identification
  • Risk assessment
  • Assignment of ownership
  • Implementation of controls
  • Ongoing monitoring
  • Periodic review

Risk categories

Risks may include:

  • Information security risks
  • Data protection risks
  • Operational risks
  • Technology risks
  • Supplier risks
  • Business continuity risks
  • Legal and regulatory risks
  • Strategic risks

Risk assessment

Risks are assessed using defined criteria that typically consider:

  • Likelihood
  • Potential impact
  • Existing controls
  • Residual risk
  • Treatment plans

Risk assessments may be performed:

  • Periodically
  • During project delivery
  • Before significant service changes
  • Following security incidents
  • Following material business changes

Risk treatment

Risk treatment activities may include:

  • Risk reduction through controls
  • Risk avoidance
  • Risk transfer
  • Risk acceptance through documented approvals

Actions are tracked through to completion and subject to management oversight.

Compliance management

Compliance programme

We maintain a compliance programme designed to identify, monitor and manage applicable legal, regulatory and contractual obligations.

The programme supports compliance with obligations relating to areas such as:

  • Data protection and privacy
  • Information security
  • Intellectual property
  • Contractual obligations
  • Employment legislation
  • Records management
  • Accessibility requirements

Policy framework

Documented policies and procedures are maintained and reviewed periodically.

Typical policy areas include:

  • Information security
  • Data protection
  • Access control
  • Acceptable use
  • Incident management
  • Business continuity
  • Change management
  • Supplier management
  • Secure software development

Compliance monitoring

Compliance may be monitored through:

  • Internal reviews
  • Control assessments
  • Management oversight
  • Vulnerability assessments
  • Audit activities
  • Supplier reviews
  • Corrective action tracking

Audit and assurance

Independent assurance activities may include:

  • Internal audits
  • External audits
  • Penetration testing
  • Certification assessments
  • Customer due diligence reviews

Findings are tracked and managed through formal remediation processes.

Change management

Change management objectives

We operate a change management process designed to ensure that changes are assessed, approved, implemented and reviewed in a controlled manner.

The objectives of change management are to:

  • Reduce service disruption
  • Maintain security and compliance
  • Protect service availability
  • Improve traceability and accountability
  • Support reliable product delivery

Types of change

Changes are generally categorised according to their nature and risk.

Examples may include:

Standard changes

Pre-approved, low-risk and repeatable activities that follow established implementation procedures.

Examples:

  • Scheduled infrastructure maintenance
  • Routine configuration updates
  • Automated deployments with established controls

Normal changes

Changes requiring risk assessment and approval before implementation.

Examples:

  • New functionality
  • Architectural changes
  • Database modifications
  • Integration enhancements

Emergency changes

Changes implemented to address critical incidents, security vulnerabilities or urgent operational issues.

Emergency changes are subject to retrospective review and approval.

Change lifecycle

Changes typically follow a documented lifecycle consisting of:

  1. Change request
  2. Impact assessment
  3. Risk assessment
  4. Review and approval
  5. Testing and validation
  6. Deployment
  7. Post-implementation review
  8. Closure and record retention

Change assessment

Prior to implementation, changes may be evaluated for:

  • Security impact
  • Privacy impact
  • Customer impact
  • Availability impact
  • Compliance impact
  • Operational readiness
  • Rollback capability

Testing requirements

Changes are expected to undergo appropriate testing before deployment.

Testing may include:

  • Unit testing
  • Integration testing
  • System testing
  • Regression testing
  • Security testing
  • User acceptance testing

The level of testing is determined according to change risk and complexity.

Release management

Changes are normally deployed using planned release processes.

Release activities may include:

  • Release planning
  • Deployment approval
  • Communication activities
  • Validation checks
  • Release monitoring

Post-implementation review

Significant changes may undergo post-implementation review to assess:

  • Achievement of objectives
  • Unexpected impacts
  • Incident occurrence
  • Required corrective actions
  • Lessons learned

Issue and corrective action management

Issues identified through audits, incidents, testing activities or customer feedback may be managed through a corrective action process.

Activities include:

  • Identification
  • Assessment
  • Assignment of ownership
  • Remediation planning
  • Verification of completion
  • Closure

Management reporting may be used to monitor progress and effectiveness.

Supplier governance

Third-party suppliers that support service delivery may be subject to governance activities including:

  • Due diligence assessments
  • Contract review
  • Security assessment
  • Performance monitoring
  • Periodic review

The level of oversight is determined by supplier criticality and risk.

Continuous improvement

Governance processes are reviewed regularly to identify opportunities for improvement.

Inputs to continual improvement include:

  • Audit findings
  • Risk assessments
  • Security events
  • Customer feedback
  • Service metrics
  • Compliance reviews
  • Lessons learned
  • Industry best practices

Additional assurance information

Subject to appropriate confidentiality arrangements, we may provide additional governance information during procurement or due diligence activities, including:

  • Risk management methodology
  • Compliance control mappings
  • Security policies
  • Audit summaries
  • Penetration testing summaries
  • Change management procedures
  • Supplier assurance documentation

## Purpose

This document provides an overview of our business continuity and disaster recovery (BCDR) capabilities for customers, partners and prospective purchasers.

The objective of our BCDR programme is to minimise the impact of operational disruptions and to support the continued delivery and recovery of services in accordance with defined business priorities.

This overview is intended for information purposes and does not form part of any contractual commitment unless explicitly stated within customer agreements.

## Scope

This document applies to:

- Production SaaS services
- Supporting cloud infrastructure
- Customer data processing environments
- Service management and support functions
- Internal business operations required to deliver the service

## Governance

Business continuity and disaster recovery activities are managed within our wider risk management and operational governance framework.

Key governance activities include:

- Identification and assessment of business continuity risks
- Definition of recovery objectives
- Maintenance of continuity and recovery plans
- Regular plan review and testing
- Management reporting and oversight
- Continuous improvement activities

## Business continuity strategy

Our business continuity strategy is designed to ensure that critical services can continue, or can be restored within agreed recovery targets, following a disruptive event.

The strategy addresses scenarios including:

- Cloud service outages
- Infrastructure failures
- Cybersecurity incidents
- Network disruptions
- Loss of supplier services
- Loss of facilities
- Workforce disruption
- Major environmental events

## Disaster recovery strategy

Disaster recovery procedures are maintained for systems supporting the delivery of the SaaS platform.

Recovery arrangements may include:

- Recovery from backups
- Infrastructure redeployment
- Restoration of application services
- Database recovery processes
- Service validation and testing
- Controlled return to normal operations

Recovery procedures are documented and reviewed periodically.

## Recovery objectives

Recovery objectives are established according to service criticality and risk assessment.

Typical service targets are shown below.

| Objective | Target |
|------------|----------|
| Recovery time objective (RTO) | ≤ 8 hours |
| Recovery point objective (RPO) | ≤ 1 hour |
| Service availability target | 99.9% monthly uptime |
| Critical incident response | Immediate triage and escalation |

Actual recovery targets may vary depending on the service component and contractual commitments.

## Data backup and recovery

Customer data is protected through backup and recovery processes designed to support operational resilience.

Backup controls include:

- Automated backups
- Encrypted backup storage
- Retention policies
- Monitoring of backup operations
- Periodic restoration testing

Backup schedules are designed to support recovery objectives and business requirements.

## Cloud infrastructure resilience

The SaaS platform is hosted within cloud infrastructure designed to provide resilience against component failure.

Resilience measures may include:

- Redundant infrastructure components
- Multi-availability-zone deployment
- Load balancing
- Automated monitoring and alerting
- Infrastructure-as-code deployment practices
- Capacity management and scaling controls

Specific architectural details are not publicly disclosed for security reasons.

## Cyber incident resilience

Business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements are integrated with incident response processes.

In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, established procedures support:

- Incident containment
- Investigation and analysis
- Service recovery
- Customer communications
- Regulatory reporting where required
- Post-incident corrective actions

## Supplier dependency management

The delivery of our services may depend upon a number of carefully selected suppliers and service providers.

Supplier resilience is considered through:

- Supplier due diligence
- Contractual controls
- Service level monitoring
- Risk assessments
- Business continuity reviews

Dependencies on critical suppliers are considered within continuity planning activities.

## Incident management and communications

A formal incident management process is maintained to support service restoration and customer communications.

The process includes:

- Incident classification
- Escalation procedures
- Technical response coordination
- Executive oversight for major incidents
- Customer notification and updates
- Post-incident review

Customers may receive updates through:

- Service status pages
- Email notifications
- Customer support channels
- Designated account contacts

## Testing and exercising

Business continuity and disaster recovery plans are tested on a regular basis.

Testing activities may include:

- Documentation review
- Tabletop exercises
- Disaster recovery simulations
- Backup restoration testing
- Incident response exercises
- Technical recovery validation

Issues identified during testing are tracked and addressed through continual improvement activities.

## Continuous improvement

The business continuity and disaster recovery programme is subject to ongoing review.

Inputs to continuous improvement include:

- Audit findings
- Incident reviews
- Risk assessments
- Customer feedback
- Technology changes
- Regulatory developments
- Testing outcomes

Updates are incorporated into plans, procedures and controls as appropriate.

## Customer responsibilities

Customers are encouraged to maintain their own business continuity arrangements.

Customer responsibilities may include:

- Managing user access and authentication
- Maintaining local copies of exported data where required
- Defining internal operational procedures
- Establishing continuity arrangements for customer-managed integrations
- Training users on contingency procedures

Business continuity is a shared responsibility between the service provider and the customer.
